Getting started

What is Grrab

Grrab is an on-page image prep tool. It lets you get images ready to use as you browse.

Most images you find on the web aren't usable as they are:

  • A watermark sits across the image.
  • The background has to go.
  • Something in the frame doesn't belong there.
  • It's smaller than you need it to be.

Fixing any of that normally means saving the file, opening another app, exporting it, and going back to where you were. The image has to leave the page before it can become usable.

Grrab moves that step to where you found the image. Hover it, press your shortcut, and fix what's off right there on the page — no saving, no second app, no going back and forth. What lands on your clipboard is the version you actually wanted.

Everything you copy is also kept in a library you can return to, and the shortcut works on any site.

Install in 30 seconds

  1. Add Grrab from the Chrome Web Store.
  2. Pin the Grrab icon to your toolbar.
  3. Click the icon and check that Grrab is on.

That's the setup. Hover any image and press Cmd+C (Ctrl+C on Windows and Linux) — see Your first copy.

Copying works right away with no account. Signing in with Google from the popup adds Editor mode and your library, and you can do it any time.

Using Grrab

Your first copy

Move your cursor over any image on any page — a purple glow shows which one the shortcut will act on.

Press Cmd+C (Ctrl+C on Windows and Linux) and Grrab takes it from there.

What happens next depends on the mode you're in. Switch between them in the popup or the side panel.

Image copied
⌘C ⌘V

Instant mode copies the image straight to your clipboard. Nothing opens, nothing interrupts you, and you carry on browsing. This is the default, and it's what you want when the image is already fine as it is.

Editor mode opens the image on the page first, so you can fix what's off before it reaches your clipboard. The page stays where it is behind the editor — nothing is downloaded and you don't leave the tab.

  • Remove Background — Keeps the subject and drops everything behind it, leaving transparency.
  • Remove Watermark — Clears a watermark laid across the whole image.
  • Erase — Brush or drag a box over anything you don't want. The area is filled in with what should have been behind it.
  • Upscale — Enlarges the image four times in each direction.

Back undoes your last step and Refresh returns the image to how it started. When it looks right, Copy puts it on your clipboard.

Editor mode requires signing in. Remove Background and Remove Watermark run on a server; Erase and Upscale run on your own machine — see Privacy and data.

Grrab only acts when your cursor is over an image. Anywhere else on the page, Cmd+C behaves exactly as it always has, so selecting text and copying it still works the way you expect.

Your library

Everything you copy while signed in is kept in your library. You don't save anything — copying is the save.

Open it from the side panel. Images are grouped by day and labeled by where they came from, so a Pinterest pin is easy to tell apart from a YouTube thumbnail weeks later.

From any image you can:

  • Put it back on your clipboard in one click.
  • Download it to your computer.
  • Open it in the editor using the link icon on the card.
  • Hover a card and press your shortcut to re-copy it (Instant mode) or open it in the editor (Editor mode).
  • Delete it, on its own or in a selection.

Downloads go wherever you've set your destination — a folder on your computer, Google Drive, or your Downloads folder — in the format you've chosen. Both are in the side panel, and you can change them at any time.

Three separate things, worth keeping apart: your clipboard holds the last image you copied, your library keeps a record of all of them, and a download is a file on your computer. Copying always fills the first. The second needs you to be signed in. The third only happens when you ask for it.

Settings

All settings

Adjust how Grrab behaves to fit the way you work.

In the popup:

  • On / off — Turns Grrab off without uninstalling it. Cmd+C goes back to normal copying while it's off.
  • Shortcut — The keys that copy the image under your cursor. Cmd+C by default.
  • Mode — Copy right away, or open the editor first. Instant by default. See Instant and Editor modes.

In the side panel:

  • Account — Sign in or out with Google. Signing in turns on your library and Editor mode.
  • Saving — Where downloads land and in what format: a folder, Google Drive, or Downloads, as PNG or JPG. PNG keeps transparency. See Where your images are saved.

Reference

Privacy and data

Grrab is designed to keep your data — and your workflow — your own.

AI upscaling runs entirely on your device: the model is bundled with the extension and executes in your browser, so no image is ever uploaded for upscaling.

When you clip without signing in, nothing about the image, the page, or you leaves your browser. Grrab sends one empty request that counts that a clip happened — no URL, no page, no image, no account or device identifier — so we can see overall usage. It can be turned off.

When you sign in, your clip history is stored on Grrab’s backend under your account. Remove BG and Remove Watermark send images to service providers only when you choose those tools — the Privacy Policy names them.

Grrab doesn’t use third-party analytics or trackers, doesn’t monitor your browsing history, and only acts on images you interact with. For the full breakdown, see the Privacy Policy.

Troubleshooting
