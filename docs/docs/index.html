Getting started

What is Grrab

Grrab is a Chrome extension that lets you copy any image on the web in one shortcut — already usable.

Hover an image, press Cmd+C, and the image lands on your clipboard. Grrab improves the quality on the way, so what you paste isn't the small preview the page was serving.

When you need more than that — a background removed, a watermark erased, a larger version — you can do it on the page, without saving the file or opening another tool. Everything you copy is also kept in a library you can come back to.

Grrab works on any site. If an image is on your screen, the shortcut works on it.

Install in 30 seconds

  1. Add Grrab from the Chrome Web Store.
  2. Pin the Grrab icon to your toolbar so you can reach the settings and the library.
  3. Click the icon once to check that Grrab is on. Everything else is already set up.

Copying to your clipboard works immediately, with no account.

Signing in with Google adds:

  • A library where everything you copy is kept — browse it, re-copy from it, or save it to a folder or Drive.
  • Editor mode, where you can adjust an image before it reaches your clipboard.

You can sign in later from the popup.

Your first copy

Move your cursor over any image on any page — a purple glow shows which one the shortcut will act on. Press the shortcut and it's on your clipboard, ready to paste.

Image copied
⌘C ⌘V

Grrab only acts when your cursor is over an image. Anywhere else on the page, Cmd+C behaves exactly as it always has, so selecting text and copying it still works the way you expect.

That's the whole loop. Everything else in this guide is about what you can do inside it.

Using Grrab

Instant and Editor modes

The mode decides what happens after you press Cmd+C. You switch between them in the popup or the side panel, and the setting stays until you change it.

Instant copies right away. The image goes to your clipboard and you carry on. This is the default.

Editor opens the image in an overlay on the page first. The page stays where it is behind it — nothing is downloaded, and you don't leave the tab. Editor mode requires signing in.

Three things you can do there:

  • Remove BG cuts out the subject and drops the background, leaving transparency behind it.
  • Erase removes anything you don't want. Brush over it or drag a box around it, and the area is filled in with what should have been behind it. Use it for watermarks, logos, captions, or objects in the way.
  • Upscale enlarges the image on your device. Small sources are enlarged more, so the result is usable however small the original was.

Back undoes your last step and Refresh returns the image to how it started. When you're done, Copy puts the result on your clipboard.

Remove BG runs on a server and needs you to be signed in. Erase and Upscale run on your own machine — see Privacy and data.

Your library

Everything you copy while signed in is kept in your library. You don't save anything — copying is the save.

Open it from the side panel. Images are grouped by day and labeled by where they came from, so a Pinterest pin is easy to tell apart from a YouTube thumbnail weeks later.

From any image you can:

  • Put it back on your clipboard in one click.
  • Download it to your computer.
  • Open the page you found it on.
  • Delete it, on its own or in a selection.

Downloads go wherever you've set your destination — a folder on your computer, Google Drive, or your Downloads folder — in the format you've chosen. Both are in the side panel, and you can change them at any time.

Three separate things, worth keeping apart: your clipboard holds the last image you copied, your library keeps a record of all of them, and a download is a file on your computer. Copying always fills the first. The second needs you to be signed in. The third only happens when you ask for it.

Settings

All settings

Adjust how Grrab behaves to fit the way you work.

In the popup:

  • On / off — Turns Grrab off without uninstalling it. Cmd+C goes back to normal copying while it's off.
  • Shortcut — The keys that copy the image under your cursor. Cmd+C by default.
  • Mode — Copy right away, or open the editor first. Instant by default. See Instant and Editor modes.
  • Output size — The size of the image that lands on your clipboard. Auto by default.

In the side panel:

  • Account — Sign in or out with Google. Signing in turns on your library and Editor mode.
  • Saving — Where downloads land and in what format: a folder, Google Drive, or Downloads, as PNG or JPG. PNG keeps transparency. See Where your images are saved.

Privacy and data

Grrab is designed to keep your data — and your workflow — your own.

AI upscaling runs entirely on your device: the model is bundled with the extension and executes in your browser, so no image is ever uploaded for upscaling.

When you clip without signing in, nothing about the image, the page, or you leaves your browser. Grrab sends one empty request that counts that a clip happened — no URL, no page, no image, no account or device identifier — so we can see overall usage. It can be turned off.

When you sign in, your clip history is stored on Grrab’s own backend under your account — never shared with third parties.

Grrab doesn’t use third-party analytics or trackers, doesn’t monitor your browsing history, and only acts on images you interact with. For the full breakdown, see the Privacy Policy.

Troubleshooting

Grrab is a small extension with a simple job, and most of the time it just works. If something isn’t behaving the way it should, or you have a question that isn’t covered here, we’d like to know.
